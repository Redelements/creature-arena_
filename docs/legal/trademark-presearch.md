# Trademark pre-search for "Ready 4 Rambles" (2026-10-28)

A desk search by Claude at Kevin's request, before any Steam page or marketing. It is not a
clearance opinion: the official registries (USPTO, EUIPO, UK IPO, WIPO) could not be queried
from here, only secondary sources and store fronts. Treat the result as "what to tell the
lawyer", and run the registry searches linked at the end in a browser before filing.

## The finding that matters

**"Ready 4 Rambles" is a sound-alike of the "Ready to Rumble" family of marks, which are
owned and actively enforced, and which have already been licensed for a fighting game.**

- "LET'S GET READY TO RUMBLE" is a registered US mark (Reg. No. 2405492, class 41,
  entertainment services), owned by Michael Buffer / Ready To Rumble, Inc. (Buffer himself
  calls it "a registered trademark exclusive to Ready To Rumble, Inc."). Related marks found:
  READY TO RUMBLE (Reg. No. 2416799), ARE YOU READY TO RUMBLE?, GET READY TO RUMBLE, and
  READY 2 RUMBLE (Reg. No. 2606845, clothing and advertising; secondary sources disagree on
  whether it is still live).
- The phrase was the title of **Ready 2 Rumble Boxing** (Midway, 1999) and its sequel: a
  fighting game whose title came from Buffer's catchphrase. So "Ready 2/To Rumble" already
  means "a fighting game" to the public, which is exactly the likelihood-of-confusion test.
- The owners sue: record labels (1997, a restraining order), film studios (New Line, Columbia,
  Disney), radio hosts and a hockey team; reported settlements from a few thousand to a few
  hundred thousand dollars. Bruce Buffer has said the family is "in and out of court each
  month" over unauthorised uses.
- "Ready 4 Rambles" differs by one vowel sound and a number. Spoken, in a trailer or by a
  streamer, it is "ready for rumbles". For a brawler, a lawyer would call that a risk of a
  confusion or dilution claim, and the first marketing push is when such owners notice.

**What this means:** keep the name only with a lawyer's written view that it is clear;
otherwise rename before the Steam page, while nothing public points at it. A rename today
costs a word in the README, the title screen's wordmark (generated by `tools/gen_logo.py`) and
the memory notes; after a Steam page it costs wishlists.

"Rambles" on its own, "Ramble" and "Rumble" are common English words used in many titles
(rambles.net, "We Ramble", Rumble streaming, "Rumble Fish", Rumble Roses); none of those is a
conflict by itself. The problem is only the "Ready [number] R-mble" shape.

## If renaming: what to avoid, and how to check a candidate

- Avoid anything of the shape "Ready … Rumble/Ramble", "Let's get …", and "… Rumble" for a
  fighting game.
- Avoid words trademarked for games by big owners: Smash, Brawlhalla, Rivals (Rivals of
  Aether), Rumble (several), Arena (generic, but "Creature Arena" style names are crowded),
  Pokémon-adjacent words (the Game Plan's rule already).
- For each candidate: the exact phrase on the web; Steam, itch.io, Google Play, App Store
  searches; USPTO, EUIPO and UK IPO searches in classes 9 (software/games) and 41
  (entertainment services), including sound-alikes; the .com and the social handles.
- A made-up or two-word mark that is not descriptive of a brawler registers more easily than a
  descriptive one.

## Registry searches to run in a browser (ten minutes)

- USPTO Trademark Search: <https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/> (search "rambles", "ready 4 rambles",
  "ready to rumble"; classes 009 and 041; look at live marks and the owners' other marks).
- USPTO TSDR for the Buffer marks' current status: <https://tsdr.uspto.gov/> (Reg. 2405492,
  2416799, 2606845).
- EUIPO eSearch plus: <https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/> (same terms, Nice classes 9, 41).
- UK IPO: <https://trademarks.ipo.gov.uk/ipo-tmtext>.
- WIPO Global Brand Database: <https://branddb.wipo.int/> (covers many registries at once,
  including sound-alike search).
- Steam: <https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=> and the Steamworks "app name" check
  when the page is created; Google Play and the App Store from a phone.
